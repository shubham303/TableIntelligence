# Outreach templates & principles

Fill in the brackets. Keep it simple and human. The operator reviews and sends by hand.

**Core lesson (learned the hard way):** the earlier "teardown" emails that diagnosed the
prospect's business ("your dashboard probably doesn't attribute…") were too clever and too
complicated — hard to read, presumptuous, and easy to be wrong about. The winning style is
the opposite: **plainly state who you are and where you can help, then get out of the way.**
Don't guess their specific pain — they may have a completely different data problem. Convey
capability and openness, not a verdict.

**Adapt every email to the specific company.** The skeleton below is constant, but rewrite
three things per prospect from what you learned qualifying them: (1) the **observation** —
their real channels/model; (2) the **data you name** — match their vertical (ecommerce →
*sales and customer data*; nonprofit → *donation and program data*; SaaS → *usage and
subscription data*; agency → *campaign and client data*); (3) the **subject + payoff** — the
benefit that matters to *this* business (growth, retention, fundraising efficiency, …). Never
ship the generic "your data" when you can name theirs.

## Reusable principles (apply to every message)

1. **Lead with the help, not your résumé.** Open with a light observation about their
   business, then frame the pain around **their time** — "If … finding clear, actionable
   insights takes up too much of your time, I can help." Only *after* that state who you are:
   "I'm a data scientist, and I build AI tools that look at sales and customer data to
   highlight the trends that actually matter for your growth." Problem first, credentials
   second. Do **not** analyze or critique their business — you'll often be wrong and it reads
   as presumptuous.
2. **Make the capability concrete and outcome-led.** Name *their* data (sales, customer,
   orders) and the payoff — **the trends that matter for growth** — not the abstract "extract
   insights from data." Concrete beats generic every time.
3. **Frame the pain as time, not struggle.** "…takes up too much of your time" lands better
   than "is a struggle," and avoid the four-verb laundry list — pick one clean pain.
4. **Name both services.** Lead with data insights, then add the automation offer as **one**
   short line: "I also build AI agents that automate the manual, repetitive work that eats your
   time." Two services, both stated — data insights **and** AI-agent automation. Keep it to one
   line so the email stays tight.
5. **Say "service"/"I can help", not "product".** You *help* / *provide a service*, never
   "built a product".
6. **No pricing in the body.** The ask is a question close — "Open to a quick chat about how
   to get more value out of your data?" Model and pricing come out on the call or the website.
7. **The website does the convincing — mid-body, as proof.** Because the pitch is deliberately
   simple, your credibility hub carries the weight. Put it in the body ("My website showcases
   how I do this") and it's the only link — every other profile is reachable from it.
8. **Warm + phone-readable.** Light observation, one offer, concrete capability, website,
   question close, "Best, Shubham." No jargon. A benefit subject earns the open.

## Cold email — capability-led template (default)

The motion is **observation → time-framed offer → concrete capability → website-as-proof →
question close**. Warm, specific, benefit-led. Say **"I can help"**, never "I built a product".

Subject: `[benefit/topic, e.g. "Making sense of your store's data"]`

```
Hi [name],

You run [light observation — their channels/model, e.g. "an independent roastery selling
direct"]—which means your business generates a lot of data. If pulling that information
together and finding clear, actionable insights from it takes up too much of your time,
I can help.

I'm a data scientist, and I build AI tools that look at [their data, e.g. "sales and customer
data"] to highlight the trends that actually matter for your growth. I also build AI agents
that automate the manual, repetitive work that eats your time.

My website (shubhamrandive.com) showcases how I do this.

Open to a quick chat about how to get more value out of your [store's/business's] data?

Best,
Shubham
```

**Why this shape works (the principles baked into it):**
- **Benefit subject, not a generic ask.** "Making sense of your store's data" names the topic
  and the payoff — it beats "Help with your data," which reads like every other cold email.
- **Frame the pain around time, not struggle.** "…takes up too much of your time" is concrete
  and empathetic; avoid the old laundry-list ("pulling it together, analyzing it, building
  reports, getting insights…"). One clean pain, not four.
- **Make the capability concrete and outcome-led.** Name *their* data (sales & customer data)
  and the payoff (**trends that matter for growth**) — not the abstract "extract insights from
  data." Concrete beats generic.
- **Let the website do proof, mid-body.** "My website showcases how I do this" gives a reason
  to click — stronger than a bare signature link.
- **Close with a question.** "Open to a quick chat…?" invites a reply better than "happy to talk."
- **Warm human sign-off.** "Best, Shubham" — not a formal block.

**Open with one light observation (do this every time — it beats a fully generic email).**
The first line should be a single factual thing you could only know by looking at their site —
their channels or model, kept **light, not too specific**. Aim for the sweet spot:

- ✅ Sweet spot (light, factual): "You run an independent roastery selling direct—which means
  your business generates a lot of data."
- ⚠️ Too generic (no proof you looked): "If your team is struggling with a lot of data…"
- ❌ Too specific / presumptuous: listing their exact verticals in detail, or a verdict like
  "your dashboard probably doesn't attribute correctly."

Rule: an **observation, never a diagnosis**; channel/model level, not a data dump. It costs
~30 seconds per prospect and flips the email from "mass mail" to "he actually looked at us."

**The AI-agent automation service (second offer — optional line).** Data insights is the lead.
When automation is a natural fit for the prospect, add **one** short line after the capability
sentence — e.g. "I also build AI agents to automate repetitive tasks, if that's ever a
pain point." Keep it optional and to one line; don't turn the email into a menu, and drop it
entirely if it makes the email feel busy. Two services exist; the email leads with one.

Notes:
- **No teardown finding in the body.** Earlier drafts embedded a public-data "insight" — drop
  it. If a prospect replies, *that's* when you get specific about their actual data.
- **The website is the only link, and it lives in-body + sign-off.** The site is live at
  **shubhamrandive.com** — no other link (no LinkedIn, Calendly, Medium, Twitter, etc.).
  Every other profile is reachable from the site.
- **No pricing / sample-then-paid line in the body** — the ask is just the question close.
- **No methodology explainer, no trust-objection line.** Offer to strip personal data only
  if they ask.
- **Vary the subject across a batch.** Identical subjects on many sends read as mass-mail —
  e.g. "Making sense of your store's data", "Getting more from your sales data", "The trends
  hiding in your data."

**Follow-up (once, ~4–5 days later, if no reply):**
```
Hi [name] — floating this back up in case it got buried. If getting more out of your data is
on your mind right now, happy to jump on a quick call and see if I can help. Either way, no worries.
```

## Social channels (LinkedIn, Reddit, Medium, Twitter) — not outreach

These are **not** advertising or cold-outreach channels. The operator uses them purely for
**knowledge-sharing** — writing up learnings, experiences, and things discovered while
doing the work — to build credibility over time. All of them point back to the website.
This skill does **not** draft promo posts for them; the cold email above is the only
outreach surface it produces.
