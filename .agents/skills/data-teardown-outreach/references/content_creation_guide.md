# Content creation guide — the credibility hub behind the cold email

The cold email is the outreach engine; **content is what makes it trustworthy.** A
prospect who clicks through from a cold email to a site full of real, specific ecommerce
data findings sees proof, not claims. This guide is how that content gets made — safely.

The content is a **byproduct of work already being done**: running the AI agent on data,
learning algorithms, and recording what was found. It is a ~30-min/day side activity —
**it never displaces the daily send count.** Content compounds slowly (6–12 months to
matter); cold email pays this month.

---

## The one distinction that governs everything

There are **two kinds of teardown**, and they must never be confused:

| | **Outreach teardown** (1-to-1) | **Published content** (1-to-many) |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | One founder, privately | The whole internet |
| Names the brand? | Yes | **No** (unless written permission) |
| Goes in | The cold email | The blog |
| Source | That prospect's public data | Open datasets / anonymized aggregates |

**Never turn the teardown you sent a specific prospect into a public blog post that names
them.** Even though it's built on *public* data (legally fine), publicly critiquing a
founder you're trying to sell to destroys the exact trust you're building — it reads as
"this person will air my weaknesses to the world." Keep the named, specific teardown
private to the email.

---

## Safe sources for published content

Publish only from sources where no one gets hurt:

| Safe source | Why it's fine |
|---|---|
| **Neutral open datasets** — Kaggle, UCI, government open data, Olist/Shopify public sets | Published for exactly this purpose; no real business to offend |
| **Anonymized aggregate** — "I analyzed 25 DTC skincare catalogs, here's the pattern" | No single brand named; sharing a *trend*, not a *critique* |
| **A real client, redacted + written permission** | A genuine case study ("numbers redacted at the owner's request") — strongest content, but only with explicit OK |

**The anonymized aggregate is the best route** — and not just for safety. "Here's what I
found across 25 stores in this niche" is more useful to readers, ranks better for SEO,
shows breadth of experience, and offends no one. The ethically-safe path is also the
higher-value path. Most published teardowns should be *open datasets + cross-brand
patterns*; save the named, specific teardown for the private email.

Every published teardown also **dogfoods the product** — it's live proof the AI agent
works, which beats any testimonial.

---

## Hard rules — never publish

- **Private/client data → never public**, redacted or not, without written permission.
  Once someone is a paying client and shares real sales data, that data is confidential,
  full stop.
- **Never publish PII** — customer names, emails, addresses, anything personal. That's a
  GDPR/legal issue, not just etiquette.
- **Analyze public facts; hedge claims.** Prices and catalog structure are facts (fine to
  analyze). Publishing a *false* negative claim about a named brand is a defamation risk —
  so if a brand is ever named, be accurate and neutral, never "this store is doing it
  wrong."
- **When in doubt, anonymize or ask.** The aggregate/open-dataset route sidesteps the
  whole question.

## How to publish (make one unit of work do triple duty)

1. **One teardown → one blog post → several social posts.** The analysis you run is the
   content. Don't create content separately from the work.
2. **Publish on your own site first, then syndicate** to LinkedIn / Medium / Substack /
   dev.to with a canonical "originally posted at [site]" link. Google credits your domain;
   you're not spread thin. Your site is the hub everything points back to.
3. **Social platforms are knowledge-sharing, not ads** (see the skill's outreach rule).
   Share learnings and findings; let the "get in touch" link on the site do the selling.
4. **Capture an email list from day one** (newsletter). Content then doesn't just build
   credibility — it captures warm leads who aren't ready to buy yet.
5. **A lead magnet** — a free "ecommerce data health-check checklist" or sample teardown
   PDF — converts readers into contacts.
6. **Target long-tail SEO** questions owners actually search: "why is my Shopify repeat
   rate low", "RFM analysis for DTC brands". One post answers one question.

## Tech & cost (keep it near-free)

The operator is a developer, so WordPress (recurring hosting + plugin/security upkeep) is
the wrong tool. Recommended stack, ≈ **$12/year total**:

| Piece | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Static generator (Astro / Hugo / Eleventy) on Cloudflare Pages or GitHub Pages | **$0 hosting** |
| Domain | Own it (don't build the hub on rented land) | ~$10–12/yr |
| Email list | Substack or ConvertKit free tier | $0 |

Ship minimal, then iterate: **1 page (who you are + get in touch) + 3 real teardown posts
is enough to launch.** Don't build a 20-page site before the cold email is proven to
convert.

---

## Cadence

- Content: ~30 min/day writing up a learning or finding — never at the expense of the
  daily email sends.
- One real published teardown (open dataset or aggregate) per week is plenty; depth and
  specificity beat volume.
