# Digital marketing for a data scientist — the map

You already know the algorithms (see `../data science/`). These notes teach the *other*
half: how digital marketing actually works, so you can walk into a client conversation and
map their pain to your toolkit. The goal is **not** to become a marketer — it's to
understand their world well enough to (a) speak their language, (b) know where their data
lives, and (c) recognise which problems your engine solves.

## Read in this order
| # | File | What you'll get |
|---|------|-----------------|
| 01 | funnel-and-metrics | The funnel every marketer thinks in, and the ~12 metrics that run the money. |
| 02 | channels-and-their-data | SEO, PPC, paid social, email/CRM — what each does and the data it emits. |
| 03 | analytics-stack-and-data-sources | GA4, Google Ads, Search Console, Meta, CRM — the actual tables you'll receive, and attribution. |
| 04 | where-data-science-helps | The payoff: each marketing problem → the exact algorithm that answers it. |

## The three things to hold onto
1. **Marketing is a money pipeline, not a creative art (to a client's CFO).** Spend goes in
   one end (ad budget, agency fees, tools); revenue comes out the other. Every metric in
   note 01 measures some ratio of the two. Your job is to make that pipeline legible and
   more efficient — that's why data science is worth paying for.
2. **The industry is drowning in dashboards and starving for interpretation.** Clients and
   agencies already have GA4, Google Ads, Looker Studio. They can all *see* the numbers.
   Almost none can answer *why did it change* or *what should I do* — which is precisely the
   gap notes 03, 09, and 10 of the data-science folder fill. **You are not selling
   reporting; you are selling the layer above it.**
3. **Agencies are the wedge (your chosen vertical).** An agency rebuilds the same
   report for many clients every month and bills for it. That means: repeated, templatable
   work (fits your deterministic engine), a real budget (it's their cost of doing
   business), and one deal = many end-clients of data. Notes 02–04 are written with the
   agency buyer in mind.

## The mental model to carry into every call
> A marketer moves a **metric** (note 01) on a **channel** (note 02), measured through an
> **analytics tool** (note 03). When the metric moves and they don't know *why*, or they
> want to *predict* or *cause* a move — that's your job (note 04).
