# 02 — The report & the story

A deliverable clients act on has a structure. This is a reusable template plus the
data-storytelling principles behind it. Keep it templated — a report-tier engagement is only
profitable if it takes hours, not a day (the velocity rule from your outreach playbook).

## The report template
1. **Executive summary (1 page, read on a phone).** The single headline finding, the 2–3
   supporting findings, and the recommended actions — each as one sentence with its dollar
   stakes. If the client reads only this, they got their money's worth.
2. **The findings (2–4).** Each in the "finding anatomy" (note 01): observation → so-what →
   action → caveat, with **one** clear visual. Ordered by impact, not by when you found them.
3. **What the data can't show.** The honest boundary — "public data can't reveal margin /
   repeat buyers / attribution." This both protects credibility and **tees up the next
   engagement** (the private-data paid analysis).
4. **Appendix / method (optional).** The audit trail — tests used, assumptions, sample
   sizes. Present for the skeptical analyst, out of the way for the owner. This is your
   engine's `metadata`.

## Data-storytelling principles
- **One chart, one message.** A chart should make a single point obvious in 3 seconds. If it
  needs a paragraph to interpret, it's the wrong chart. (See the `dataviz` skill for the how.)
- **Show the comparison, not the number.** "22%" means nothing alone; "22% vs 30% category
  norm vs your 26% last year" tells a story. Context is the story.
- **Annotate the insight on the chart.** Put the takeaway in the title ("Repeat rate has
  fallen 3 quarters running"), not a neutral label ("Repeat rate by quarter").
- **Order by importance, not chronology or convenience.** Lead with the finding that moves
  the most money.
- **Respect the reader's time.** Every element earns its place or gets cut. Density ≠ value.

## Matching depth to the buyer
- **Report-tier (small client):** templated, descriptive/diagnostic, a handful of findings,
  fast turnaround. Lean on RFM/repeat-rate/"what changed."
- **Retainer-tier:** more depth, forecasting/modelling/causal, a recurring monthly cadence
  where the *same* structure repeats with fresh data — which is exactly what makes the
  deterministic engine profitable to run every month.

## The recurring-report advantage
Because the structure is fixed and the numbers are engine-computed, month N+1 is mostly
"same template, new data, new prose." That low marginal cost is the entire economic case for
the productized service — and why landing one client as a *retainer* beats ten one-offs.

## A note on turnaround as a selling point
"You'll have it within days, every month, on the same clean template" is itself a pitch —
agencies rebuild reports by hand and hate it. Reliability and speed are features you sell,
not just internal efficiency.
