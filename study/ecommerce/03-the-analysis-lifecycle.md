# 03 — The analysis lifecycle of a store (first report → retainer)

Analyses have a natural order that mirrors a store's questions as it grows. This is your
engagement roadmap: what to deliver first, and what to upsell as trust and data grow.

## Stage 1 — "Show me what's happening" (the sample / first report)
Cheap, fast, works on modest data — the descriptive/diagnostic tier.
- **Data-quality pass** (note 01) — clean, flag cancellations, honest totals. Invisible but
  it's why they trust everything after.
- **Repeat-rate + RFM** (note 04) — "here are your Champions and your 40 At-Risk customers
  holding $X." Almost always a surprise; your strongest opener.
- **AOV / best-sellers / "what changed vs last period"** (notes 01, 03).
This is the **report-tier** deliverable and the free sample that lands the client.

## Stage 2 — "Tell me why" (the diagnostic report)
The interpretation layer the client can't do themselves.
- **Key-driver decomposition** (note 09) — "CVR dropped, and it's concentrated in mobile
  from one channel." Turns a number into an action.
- **Retention cohorts** (note 04) — are newer cohorts retaining better or worse?
- **Market basket** (note 06) — cross-sell/bundle rules.
- **Seasonality decomposition** (note 07) — separate real growth from the calendar.

## Stage 3 — "Tell me what's next / who to target" (the retainer)
Needs volume and history → e-commerce / retainer-tier.
- **Forecasting** (note 07) — revenue/demand next quarter with a confidence range.
- **Churn & LTV models** (note 08) — who's about to lapse, who's worth acquiring.
- **Behavioural clustering** (note 05) — personas beyond RFM.

## Stage 4 — "Prove it works" (the senior differentiator)
- **Causal attribution** (note 10) — did the redesign/campaign *cause* the lift, or was it
  seasonality? The hardest-to-refute answer, and the anchor of a long retainer.

## How to use this ladder
- **Land at Stage 1**, always — it's the sample that converts and it de-risks the relationship.
- **Upsell up the ladder** as data and trust accumulate; each stage is a natural next
  invoice.
- **Match the stage to the data** — a small store may top out at Stage 2, and that's a
  perfectly good retainer. Never sell Stage 3/4 to a store that can't feed it (the volume
  discipline). Over-promising a churn model on 300 orders is how you produce a wrong finding.

## The recurring-revenue insight
Stages 1–2 **repeat every month** (the store keeps generating data), which is exactly why a
one-off report becomes a retainer: "what changed, why, who's at risk, what's the forecast"
is a monthly cadence. Your deterministic engine makes that repetition cheap — the whole
point of the productized service.
