# Outreach templates & principles

Fill in the brackets. Always lead with the real finding from the teardown; never send a
generic template. The operator reviews and sends by hand.

## Reusable principles (apply to every message)

1. **Pitch first, proof second.** Lead with the service ("I provide a service that uses
   AI agents to analyze your ecommerce data"), then back it with **one sentence** of proof
   from their public data. The finding earns credibility; it is not the whole email.
2. **Say "service", not "product".** You *provide a service*, never "built a product".
3. **Hedge honestly.** The public-data finding is a hypothesis — "usually means," "worth a
   look." Never lecture someone about their own business.
4. **No pricing in the body.** The ask is "let's get in touch" — the service model and
   pricing come out on the call or the website, never in the cold email.
5. **The website does the convincing.** A credibility hub (WordPress blog + Medium /
   Twitter / LinkedIn all pointing back) is what makes a simple pitch land. Put the site
   in the signature; sequence the email push to ramp as the site fills with content.
6. **Short + phone-readable.** Two short paragraphs, ~4 sentences. A plain benefit-led
   subject earns the open; a long body loses it.

## Cold email — product-led template (default)

The motion is **service pitch first, one-line proof second**, backed by a credibility
website in the signature (blogs/Medium/Twitter/LinkedIn all point back to it — the site
does the convincing, not the email). Two short paragraphs, ~4 sentences. Say **"I provide
a service"**, never "I built a product".

Subject: `[plain + benefit, e.g. "Getting real insight out of your store's data"]`

```
Hi [name],

I saw you run [Brand] — and as an ecommerce brand grows, extracting real insight from
your data gets harder, more expensive, and more time-consuming. I provide a service that
uses AI agents to do exactly that: analyze your ecommerce data and surface what's actually
driving (and draining) revenue.

From the public data available about your [store / site], I found [ONE-SENTENCE insight,
tied to money or a decision]. If you think it's worth your time to see how I can help you,
let's get in touch.

[Shubham Randive](https://shubhamrandive.com) · shubhamrandive.com
```

Notes:
- **Paragraph 1 is the pitch, paragraph 2 is the hook.** The finding is *one sentence*,
  not the whole email — it earns credibility without bloating the message.
- **Only the website goes in the signature.** The site is live at **shubhamrandive.com** —
  link the clickable name to it and include **no other link** (no LinkedIn, Calendly,
  Medium, Twitter, etc.). Every other profile is reachable from the site, so the signature
  stays to one clean line.
- **No pricing / sample-then-paid line in the body** — the ask is just "let's get in
  touch"; the service model and pricing come out on the call or the website.
- **No methodology explainer, no trust-objection line.** Offer to strip personal data only
  if they ask.

**Follow-up (once, ~4–5 days later, if no reply):**
```
Hi [name] — floating this back up in case it got buried. If it's useful, happy to walk
you through what I found on a quick call. Either way, no worries.
```

## Social channels (LinkedIn, Reddit, Medium, Twitter) — not outreach

These are **not** advertising or cold-outreach channels. The operator uses them purely for
**knowledge-sharing** — writing up learnings, experiences, and things discovered while
doing the work — to build credibility over time. All of them point back to the website.
This skill does **not** draft promo posts for them; the cold email above is the only
outreach surface it produces.
