# House style guide — shubhamrandive.com blog

The reference for *how* a post should read. When in doubt, open the newest 2–3 files in
`src/content/blog/` and match them — this guide just makes the pattern explicit.

## The voice in one paragraph

A sharp, generous practitioner thinking out loud. First person, direct address, plain
words, contractions. Confident but not hype-y; it respects the reader's intelligence and
never pads. Every post makes **one** point and earns it, then ends on a line worth
remembering. It reads like a smart friend explaining something over coffee — not a
whitepaper, not a LinkedIn thought-leadership post, not a content-marketing listicle.

## Structure

- **Hook, not a definition.** Open with the problem, a concrete scene, or a claim — never
  "X is a technique that…". Examples that work:
  - *"Someone on your team has a decision to make… and almost on reflex: 'Let's just hop on a call.'"*
  - *"One of the fastest ways to understand an ecommerce business is to stop looking at totals and start looking at *who* is buying."*
- **Body = the argument.** A few sections, each moving the one idea forward. Use `##`
  headings that are themselves little claims ("The meeting should be the last step, not the
  first."), not labels ("Background").
- **Payoff / takeaway.** Close with the durable reframe. The RFM post lands on: *"the
  method that changes the decision is often much simpler than the method that looks
  impressive."* That's the model — a sentence the reader keeps.
- **Optional CTA.** A one-line italic sign-off inviting contact via
  `mailto:randiveshubham3@gmail.com`. Keep it warm and low-pressure.

## Formatting

- **Short paragraphs** (1–4 sentences). White space is a feature.
- **Bold** carries the argument's spine — one or two phrases per section, no more.
- **Italics** for emphasis and quoted phrases; **`inline code`** for technical terms,
  functions, file names.
- **Lists only for real enumerations** (e.g. the three RFM segments). Never turn an
  argument into bullets to avoid writing the connective prose.
- **Blockquotes** for the privacy note (analysis posts) and for a rule/principle you want
  to isolate.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Make one point and drive it home | Cover five loosely related things |
| Open with a hook or problem | Open with a dictionary definition |
| Use concrete, specific examples | Speak in abstractions and buzzwords |
| Say what's uncertain plainly | Oversell or imply false precision |
| Let prose do the work | Hide thin thinking behind bullet lists |
| End on a memorable reframe | Trail off or end with "In conclusion," |
| Keep it as short as the idea allows | Pad to hit a word count |

## Analysis-post specifics

- Lead with the **public-data blockquote** disclaimer.
- Show the *shape* of the finding and why it matters for a decision — not a methods dump.
  Readers care about the "so what", with just enough method to trust it.
- It's fine (and on-brand) to point out that the simple method beat the impressive one.
- Never publish private/client data or invented numbers. Reproduce on open data and say so.

## Titles & descriptions

- **Title:** specific and honest, sentence case, no clickbait. Good: *"What RFM
  segmentation reveals on a public ecommerce dataset."* Bad: *"5 SHOCKING Truths About
  Your Customers."*
- **Description:** one line the listing/meta uses — say what the reader *gets*, not what
  the post *is about*.

## Anti-patterns to delete on sight

- "In today's fast-paced world…" and any throat-clearing intro.
- "Let's dive in", "Without further ado", "It's important to note that".
- Emoji-bulleted feature lists; SEO keyword stuffing.
- A duplicate `# Title` H1 in the body (the layout already renders the title).
- Self-linking to shubhamrandive.com from inside the site.
